# Investor Frameworks and Sources

This file saves the fundraising and narrative frameworks used to rewrite the 360Engage investor memo. It is not a pitch itself. It is the source pack behind the pitch.

## Why This Exists

The original roadmap draft contained useful scope and budget thinking, but it read more like an internal delivery document than an investor-grade narrative. To improve it, the rewrite uses a blend of:

- venture pitch structure
- founder storytelling discipline
- market timing logic
- traction and evidence framing
- operator credibility for sports-club buyers

## Core Frameworks Used

### 1. Sequoia: Business Plan / Pitch Structure

Source:
- [Sequoia - Writing a Business Plan](https://sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)

Key structure pulled from Sequoia:

1. Company purpose
2. Problem
3. Solution
4. Why now
5. Market potential
6. Competition / alternatives
7. Business model
8. Team
9. Financials
10. Vision

How it is applied to 360Engage:

- `Company purpose`: define 360Engage in one sentence without slipping into a feature list.
- `Problem`: clubs are commercially blind to a large share of supporter behavior, especially outside ticketing and app logins.
- `Solution`: a club-owned engagement and transaction layer spanning game-day and off-game interactions.
- `Why now`: AI interaction quality, browser-first experiences, fan expectations, and club commercial pressure have all converged.
- `Market potential`: first sports clubs, then multi-club data and workflow infrastructure.
- `Competition`: point solutions, club apps, generic chatbots, disconnected commerce tools, and agencies.
- `Business model`: club SaaS plus optional performance-linked structures where needed.
- `Team`: founder technical credibility plus commercial and club-market relationships.
- `Financials`: phased capital deployment rather than vague “big vision” spending.
- `Vision`: the club-owned intelligence and commerce operating layer, not a novelty chatbot.

### 2. Y Combinator: Seven Questions

Source:
- [YC - How to Pitch Your Company](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/)

YC’s seven questions:

1. What do you do?
2. How big is the market?
3. What’s your progress?
4. What’s your unique insight?
5. What’s your business model?
6. Who’s on your team?
7. What do you want?

Key YC guidance preserved:

- explain the company simply
- avoid jargon and inflated language
- make progress legible
- own a simple business model
- make the ask explicit

How it is applied to 360Engage:

- `What do you do?`: 360Engage helps clubs turn supporter interactions into revenue, service resolution, and first-party fan data across game-day and off-game journeys.
- `Market`: use bottom-up logic where possible rather than hand-wavy TAM inflation.
- `Progress`: current demos, structured workflows, investor and club conversations, LOI path, and explicit MVP sequencing.
- `Unique insight`: clubs often know who bought the ticket, but not what that supporter actually did, wanted, bought, asked, or nearly bought.
- `Business model`: keep the model simple enough to understand quickly, with flexibility only as a commercial option.
- `Ask`: capital to turn demos and pilot interest into club-grade software and repeatable deployments.

### 3. YC: Short Pitch / “Powerful Facts” Framing

Source:
- [YC - Practical Design: Pitching](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/practical-design-pitching/)

Useful idea:

- investors remember a few “powerful facts”, not every feature

The memo therefore repeats a few core facts on purpose:

1. Clubs are under-monetizing and under-learning from supporter interactions.
2. 360Engage gives clubs a direct operating layer across service, commerce, and data.
3. The company has two complementary product surfaces, not one:
   - game-day QR/PWA companion
   - voice / concierge AI layer
4. The wedge can be phased, but the platform ambition is larger.

### 4. Andreessen Horowitz: Fundraising Process Reality

Source:
- [a16z - 16 Common Questions About Fundraising](https://a16z.com/16-common-questions-about-fundraising/)

Key takeaway used:

- fundraising is a process, and founders need a narrative that is ready before they feel perfectly ready

How it is applied:

- the new memo is designed to be used in live conversations, not just as a background document
- it includes an explicit financing rationale, use-of-funds logic, and what must be proven at the next milestone

### 5. Andreessen Horowitz: Metrics Mindset

Source:
- [a16z - Introducing a16z Growth’s Guide to Growth Metrics](https://a16z.com/2022/12/14/introducing-a16z-growths-guide-to-growth-metrics/)

Key idea used:

- companies need a coherent metrics layer that shows growth, efficiency, and signal quality

How it is applied:

- the memo names the system not only as an experience layer, but as a measurement and operating layer
- investor-facing examples focus on:
  - conversion by journey
  - commerce per supporter
  - service-resolution outcomes
  - first-party data capture
  - sponsor activation evidence
  - repeatability across clubs

### 6. NFX: Why Now / Timing

Source:
- [NFX - Why Startup Timing is Everything](https://www.nfx.com/post/why-startup-timing-is-everything)

Framework used:

Timing improves when three conditions line up:

1. enabling technology
2. economic impetus
3. cultural acceptance

How it is applied to 360Engage:

- `Enabling technology`: large language models, real-time conversational systems, browser-first UX, QR-driven workflows, maturing integrations
- `Economic impetus`: clubs need higher revenue per fan, better sponsor reporting, stronger retention, and more first-party data
- `Cultural acceptance`: supporters now expect conversational, immediate, personalized digital assistance

## Practical Rules Used In The Rewrite

These writing rules are derived from the sources above and applied directly.

1. Lead with the company in one sentence.
2. Separate the wedge from the long-term platform.
3. Make “why now” explicit.
4. Use simple language over startup jargon.
5. Show a believable path from pilot to repeatability.
6. Do not pretend the demo equals finished product.
7. Treat club owners and operators as a second investor audience.
8. Translate product detail into commercial outcomes.
9. Be concrete about what is hard.
10. Make the financing ask match the phase plan.

## Specific Implications For 360Engage

The rewritten investor memo should:

- present 360Engage as a club operating layer, not just a fan app
- show that the voice demo and the game-day demo are separate surfaces inside one platform logic
- explain why the company can start with one commercial wedge without shrinking the overall opportunity
- position data as a compounding asset, but not in a hand-wavy “AI data moat” way
- show investors that the GTM story is grounded in actual club pain, not just AI novelty
- make it legible why a club would buy this now, not “someday”
