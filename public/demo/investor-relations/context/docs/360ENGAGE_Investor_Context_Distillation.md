# 360ENGAGE Investor Context Distillation

This is the compacted working note behind the investor memo. It combines the strongest signals from the current roadmap draft, older planning docs, and the relevant Stewart / Glasshouse Fathom meetings.

## One-Sentence Definition

360Engage is a club-owned supporter engagement, service, commerce, and data layer that helps sports clubs convert previously invisible fan interactions into measurable revenue, better service outcomes, and first-party intelligence.

## What The Product Actually Is

The current material consistently points to one platform with two product surfaces:

1. A game-day, browser-first, QR-triggered companion
2. A concierge-style AI interface, especially voice, for deeper and more personal fan engagement

Those are not separate companies. They are separate entry points into the same club-owned operating layer.

## What Problem It Solves

The strongest repeated problem statement is not “clubs need another app.”

It is:

- clubs do not sufficiently know or monetize what supporters do before, during, and after matches
- clubs lose revenue because purchases, questions, complaints, and intent signals are fragmented or invisible
- clubs are commercially blind to a large share of supporter behavior, especially casual, anonymous, or lightly engaged fans
- clubs also struggle to respond quickly and personally without adding headcount

This matters commercially because it touches:

- food and beverage revenue
- merchandise revenue
- ticket upgrades and future ticket sales
- sponsor activation and evidence
- service and complaint handling
- supporter retention and membership conversion

## Strongest Insights From The Meetings

### From “Stewart / Shake - 360 Engage”

Most important signals:

- the MVP must be phased and disciplined
- chat was seen as the practical initial wedge because voice was considered expensive and operationally harder at that stage
- food and beverage was discussed as a strong first wedge because it is simple and revenue-linked
- the dashboard is illustrative, but the sales story is that the backend can surface whatever clubs need to see
- LOIs matter as a bridge to funding the MVP

Important nuance:

- this meeting is strongest on sequencing and capital discipline
- it is weaker as a market-facing story because it over-rotates toward what is easiest to build

### From “360 run through”

Most important signals:

- the current hard-coded demo was seen as misaligned with the real value proposition
- the key value proposition is not generic chat, but AI-powered supporter engagement tied to commerce and data capture
- the team clearly sees a two-product story:
  - voice / concierge
  - in-stadium QR companion
- the long-term value is not just transactions, but the compounding supporter data asset and club relationship layer
- Graham and Wil emphasized urgency and external credibility risk: the demo must be strong enough to win investor and club attention fast

Most useful quote-level insight:

- clubs are “blind” to 60-70% of their supporter base

This is probably the sharpest market insight in the set.

### From “AI for Sports”

Most important signals:

- the voice AI demo became the top sales priority because it creates stronger emotional and investor response
- the presentation flow was deliberately reframed:
  - open with voice
  - follow with game-day chat
- the ideal voice scenario is a supporter planning a trip, needing tickets, hotel, merch, and contextual support
- pricing flexibility was discussed, with willingness to offer either subscription or performance-linked structures depending on buyer preference

Most useful commercial insight:

- the job of the demo is to make people feel the future product, not to pretend every module is already fully deployed

## Core Narrative That Best Fits The Evidence

The strongest coherent story is:

360Engage helps clubs turn supporter interactions into revenue, service quality, and proprietary fan intelligence. It does this through two connected engagement surfaces:

- a game-day interaction layer for QR-led, in-the-moment journeys
- a concierge AI layer for richer, higher-value, pre-match and off-match supporter interactions

The commercial wedge can start narrow, but the platform logic is broad.

## Best Investor Framing

### For Pure Investors

They will care most about:

- why this is not just another club app
- whether the market is real and large enough
- whether there is a wedge with credible adoption dynamics
- whether the product can become repeatable rather than bespoke
- whether the data layer compounds over time

### For Sports-Industry Investors

They will care most about:

- whether clubs will actually adopt it
- whether the workflows fit real matchday operations
- whether the club burden is low
- whether the product helps with sponsor value, fan engagement, and revenue per seat

### For Club Owners / Club Operators

They will care most about:

- how quickly it helps them make more money
- how little operational pain it causes
- whether it improves the fan experience without risking reputational damage
- whether they keep control of the supporter relationship and the data

## The “Why Now” Case

The strongest why-now argument is a combination of:

- better conversational AI and workflow tooling
- browser-first and QR-driven experiences that reduce app-download friction
- clubs needing new revenue per fan, not just more ticket sales
- increasing pressure for first-party data ownership
- supporter expectations for faster and more personalized digital help

## What Needs To Be Avoided In The Investor Memo

1. Over-positioning this as a generic chatbot
2. Over-positioning this as only a voice company
3. Pretending the demo equals a production product
4. Leading with architecture before business value
5. Making the product feel bespoke to one club forever
6. Claiming a data moat without first explaining how data is actually captured and used
7. Making the business model look confused

## Best Coherent Product Position

The cleanest position is:

360Engage is a club-owned supporter operating layer.

That layer spans:

- service
- commerce
- campaign orchestration
- supporter memory
- first-party data capture
- reporting and sponsor evidence

The company can then describe two initial deployment surfaces:

- `Concierge`: pre-match, off-match, and supporter relationship management
- `Game-day`: in-stadium transactions, service, issue reporting, and tactical offers

## Recommended Business Model Framing

The memo should lead with a primary model and mention flexibility second.

Recommended framing:

- primary model: annual or multi-year SaaS / platform subscription
- optional structures: implementation fees, module fees, performance-linked upside, or limited commercial sharing where strategically useful

Why:

- this keeps the company sounding like infrastructure, not a coupon affiliate
- it still leaves room for buyer-friendly deal structures

## Recommended Go-To-Market Framing

1. Win one or a few credible pilot clubs
2. Use those pilots to build proof, references, and refined operational playbooks
3. Convert from club-specific delivery into repeatable modules
4. Expand through league, agency, sponsor, and operator relationships

## Risks That Should Be Addressed Directly

Investors will trust the memo more if the hard parts are named.

Main risks:

- integration complexity across club systems
- AI reliability and liability, especially around accessibility or safety-sensitive information
- proving ROI quickly enough for clubs with limited budgets
- avoiding a bespoke-services trap
- turning demos and LOIs into production deployments

## Recommended Response To Those Risks

- phase the product
- hard-code or rules-engine critical facts where required
- start with high-value use cases rather than universal coverage
- keep club operational burden low
- use pilots to create reusable integration and deployment playbooks

## Capital Logic

The Stewart roadmap’s budget logic is useful, but the investor memo should present it more clearly:

- Phase 1 capital builds a credible live wedge and reference deployment
- Phase 2 capital improves repeatability, reliability, and transaction depth
- Phase 3 capital expands the system into a true multi-club platform

This is more persuasive than simply listing technical workstreams.

## Most Important Message To Preserve

The product is not trying to replace the club-fan relationship.

It is trying to give the club a far more intelligent, responsive, and commercial layer on top of the relationship it already owns.
