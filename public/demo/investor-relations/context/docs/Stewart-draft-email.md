I am drafting the roadmap and platform summary for our budgeting. I have been germinating a term that differentiates and enhances the story arc. I want your candid opinion on a positioning concept I created for 360ENGAGE before I push it any further.

At the highest level, the abstract of the positioning:

360ENGAGE helps teams activate the unseen fan.

By “unseen fan,” I mean the large portion of the audience that sits outside the club’s existing engagement stack. These are fans who may attend, scan, click, watch, share, respond, or buy, but are not captured inside the app, not logged in, and not properly represented in CRM data. Our view is that most current sports engagement systems are built around known users, app users, and registered users, while a significant share of actual fan value sits outside that structure.

The product advantage we are trying to define is a platform-agnostic engagement architecture. The working phrase for that is “liquid engagement,” although I want your honest reaction to the term itself.

What we mean by it is not marketing language for its own sake. The idea is that the engagement layer is not trapped inside a single app, channel, or identity state. It can move across QR, PWA micropages, SMS, WhatsApp, email, social, ticketing flows, in-stadium signage, and sponsor activations, while adapting the message and call to action to the moment, the channel, and the fan’s level of intent.

In practical terms, a fan does not need to download an app or enter a fixed journey to participate. The interaction can begin wherever the fan already is, with the right prompt delivered in the right context. That is the operating logic behind the platform-agnostic claim.

The commercial rationale is straightforward.

First, lower acquisition friction. A PWA micropage that opens instantly from a QR code or link removes the download, registration, storage, and update burden that suppresses app participation. That allows us to reach a broader casual and unregistered audience.

Second, channel portability. If the engagement logic can travel across touchpoints instead of being locked into one app experience, the model becomes easier to deploy across clubs, venues, sponsors, and campaigns. That improves scalability and reduces implementation resistance.

Third, better data capture at the edge. If the first interaction happens before formal registration, we can still generate measurable event data, intent signals, and progressive profiling. That creates value before identity is fully known and gives teams a path to monetize interaction earlier.

Our working view is that this could be a real competitive advantage because most sports engagement stacks are still structured around known users and owned channels. What we think we can own is a clearer definition of who these unseen fans are, why current systems miss them, and how 360ENGAGE captures them without app friction.

There is also some market validation around the underlying problem. Related language such as “invisible fans” is already being used to describe audiences whose activity and value are not captured by current club infrastructure. That suggests the pain point is real, even if the terminology is still loose.

What is your opinion on:

1.       Does “liquid engagement” help explain the architecture, or does it sound too soft, vague, or contrived?

2.       Is “unseen fan” a strong enough wedge to lead with?

3.       Does this feel like a meaningful product distinction, or are we still one level too abstract in how we are describing it?

My instinct is that the core business case is strong, but the language has to be tight enough to survive a skeptical operator, CTO, or investor test. I would much rather kill or refine the term now than build around something that does not hold up.

Would value your blunt reaction.
