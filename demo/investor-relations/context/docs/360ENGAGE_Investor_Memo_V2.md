# 360ENGAGE Investor Memo

## Working Title

Club-owned AI engagement and commerce infrastructure for sports organizations

## Executive Summary

Most sports clubs still operate with a surprising blind spot. They know who bought the ticket, but not enough about what that supporter wanted, asked, nearly bought, struggled with, or would have done next with the right prompt at the right moment. A family travelling in for a first match, a fan trying to order food at halftime, a supporter asking about accessibility, or someone ready to buy merch, hospitality, or next-match tickets often disappears into disconnected systems, staff inboxes, or no system at all.

360Engage is built to fix that. It gives clubs a club-owned interaction layer that turns supporter conversations and journeys into three things at once:

- better supporter experiences
- more measurable commercial outcomes
- a compounding first-party fan data asset

The platform begins with two connected product surfaces:

1. a game-day, browser-first QR companion for in-stadium journeys
2. a concierge-style AI interface, including voice, for richer pre-match and off-match engagement

Those surfaces matter because together they let a club engage supporters across the whole relationship, not just one transaction. The game-day layer improves live conversion, service, and issue handling. The concierge layer captures higher-value, higher-context interactions such as travel planning, hospitality support, first-match family moments, complaint resolution, and personalized recommendations.

The immediate business case is straightforward: help clubs make more money per supporter, support fans better, and finally capture data from interactions that are currently lost. The longer-term opportunity is larger: become the club-owned operating layer through which supporter service, commerce, segmentation, campaign activation, and fan intelligence increasingly flow.

## 1. The Story In One Page

Imagine a supporter relationship from the club’s point of view today.

The club may know:

- the supporter bought a ticket
- the supporter opened an email
- the supporter once bought a shirt

But the club usually does not know enough about what happened next.

Did that supporter try to buy food and abandon the journey? Did they want to bring a child to their first match? Did they need travel help, hospitality, or accessible route guidance? Did they ask for a refund, try to find merch, or show clear intent to buy for the next game?

That missing middle is where commercial value leaks out.

360Engage is designed to fill it.

Instead of forcing clubs to choose between a clunky app, a generic chatbot, and manual supporter service, 360Engage creates a branded, club-owned interaction layer that sits between the club and the supporter across the moments that matter.

For the supporter, it feels like the club is more responsive, more personal, and easier to buy from.

For the club, it means:

- higher revenue per supporter
- better conversion on matchday and beyond
- cleaner issue resolution
- more useful supporter profiles
- stronger sponsor activation evidence
- a more defensible first-party data asset over time

## 4. The Problem

Clubs have more supporter demand than they can currently see or manage well.

Most clubs know some combination of:

- who bought a ticket
- who is on a membership list
- who is in their CRM
- who opened a marketing email

But they often do not know enough about what their supporters actually want and do in the moments that matter:

- what they intended to buy but did not
- what help they needed on matchday
- what product or experience they would have purchased with better timing
- what questions they ask before attending
- what hospitality, travel, accessibility, or family support they need
- what sponsor or local partner offer is actually relevant to them
- what complaint or issue nearly prevented future spend

In other words, clubs frequently own the audience but not the operational layer that translates supporter behavior into revenue and intelligence.

That gap has real consequences:

- lower revenue per seat and per supporter
- weaker in-stadium conversion
- poor visibility into off-match supporter intent
- fragmented customer service
- weak sponsor reporting
- underdeveloped first-party fan data assets

## 5. The Solution

360Engage provides clubs with a supporter operating layer that spans service, commerce, and data capture.

### Surface A: Game-Day Companion

This is a browser-first, QR-triggered supporter experience designed for live matchday moments. A supporter scans a QR code in-seat or in-concourse and is taken into a fast, club-branded journey that can handle:

- food and beverage discovery or ordering
- merchandise browsing and purchase
- ticket upgrades and future match offers
- issue reporting and support
- personalized promotions and campaign prompts
- fan segmentation and data capture

The point is not just to answer questions. The point is to create actionable journeys that generate revenue, reduce friction, and leave a usable record behind.

### Surface B: Concierge AI

This is the higher-touch layer for richer supporter engagement outside the stadium or before the supporter reaches the ground. It can be deployed as voice, text, or embedded conversational support and is designed for workflows such as:

- travel and matchday logistics
- first-match family support
- hospitality and accommodation support
- merchandise pre-ordering
- service recovery and complaint handling
- supporter memory across repeat interactions

The best way to think about the concierge product is not “AI novelty.” It is a new club interface for supporter service and monetization.

### What Makes This Different

360Engage is not just:

- a club app
- a website chatbot
- a voice demo
- a QR microsite
- a dashboard

It is a connected system in which each supporter interaction can improve:

- the immediate outcome for that supporter
- the club’s commercial understanding
- the quality of future journeys
- the club’s visibility into supporter behavior

## 10. Product Architecture In Plain English

Investors do not need every technical detail, but they do need confidence that the product can evolve from pilot to platform.

The architecture is intentionally phased:

### Phase 1: MVP Validation Layer

Goal:
- prove that a low-friction supporter journey can create real utility, data capture, and commercial signal

Likely components:
- QR and browser-first entry
- core supporter workflows
- basic commerce paths
- club dashboard
- lightweight supporter profile and event history

### Phase 2: Commercial Hardening

Goal:
- make the system more reliable, less bespoke, and more sellable to additional clubs

Likely components:
- stronger integration abstraction
- better admin tools
- improved transaction reliability
- more reusable content and campaign controls

### Phase 3: Multi-Club Platform

Goal:
- turn pilot logic into repeatable infrastructure

Likely components:
- multi-club configuration
- stronger reporting and segmentation
- sponsor modules
- more mature data and automation layers

The key principle is discipline: the company should not try to build every future feature before it has proven a narrow, high-value wedge.

The important investor takeaway is that the technology story gets stronger with each phase. The company does not need full technical sophistication on day one, but the architecture should allow the system to become more configurable, more integrated, more data-rich, and more defensible over time.

## 16. Risks And How The Company Should Handle Them

### Integration Risk

Clubs use messy, inconsistent systems.

Response:
- start with narrow workflows
- build integration abstraction over time
- avoid pretending universal integration exists on day one

### AI Reliability Risk

Accessibility, safety, or operational facts cannot be left to loose generation.

Response:
- use structured, club-sourced data
- hard-code or rules-engine critical information
- reserve generative AI for the layers where it creates value safely

### Bespoke Services Risk

The company could become a custom studio instead of a software business.

Response:
- keep pilots disciplined
- standardize the highest-value journeys
- turn deployment work into repeatable templates and modules

### Sales Cycle Risk

Clubs may move slowly, budgets may be constrained, and enthusiasm may not convert automatically.

Response:
- sell a wedge with visible ROI
- make implementation low-burden
- lead with operator and commercial outcomes, not technical novelty
